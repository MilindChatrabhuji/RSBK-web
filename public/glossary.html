RSBK stands for Rasashastra and Bhaishajya Kalpana (Subject Code: AyUG-RB for BAMS Second Professional and AYPG-RB for MD/MS Ayurveda). It is the premier discipline of Ayurvedic pharmaceutical engineering, inorganic chemistry, nanomedicine, and dosage formulation design. Rasashastra deals with mercurial processing (Parada), mineral purification (Shodhana), calcination (Marana), and organo-metallic therapeutics. Bhaishajya Kalpana encompasses extraction principles (Panchavidha Kashaya), secondary dosage forms (Sneha, Asava-Arishta, Avaleha, Vati), shelf-life kinetics, and contemporary quality standards (API/AFI).

According to Rasa Ratna Samucchaya (2/1), the eight classical Maharasa (primary mineral group) are: 1. Abhraka (Mica / Biotite), 2. Vaikranta (Tourmaline / Fluorspar), 3. Makshika (Copper Pyrite / Chalcopyrite), 4. Vimala (Iron Pyrite), 5. Shilajatu (Asphaltum punjabianum), 6. Sasyaka / Tuttha (Copper Sulphate), 7. Chapala (Bismuth / Selenium Ore), and 8. Rasaka / Kharpara (Calamine / Zinc Carbonate). These minerals possess supreme potency in potentiating Parada and formulating bioavailable rejuvenation therapies.

The classical quality control tests establish that a heavy metal or mineral has been transformed into a non-toxic, biocompatible nano-particulate state:
• Varitara: When sprinkled gently onto still water, the incinerated Bhasma floats on the surface due to low density and particle fineness.
• Rekhapurna: The particles enter deep within the dermal ridges of finger creases when rubbed.
• Apunarbhava: When heated with Mitra Panchaka (Ghee, Guda, Gunja, Tankana, Madhu), metallic reduction does not occur.
• Niruttha: Heated with a piece of pure silver foil, no amalgam or alloy forms.
• Nishchandrika: Absence of any metallic specular reflection or shine under direct bright sunlight.
• Uttama: A grain of paddy or rice placed on the floating Bhasma on water does not cause it to sink.

As documented in Sharngadhara Samhita (Madhyama Khanda 1/1), the five primary dosage forms are:
1. Swarasa (Expressed Juice): Crushing fresh herbal drug and expressing through cloth (dose: 2 Pala / 48 ml).
2. Kalka (Herbal Paste): Grinding fresh or dried herb with water to a bolus (dose: 1 Karsha / 12 g).
3. Kwatha / Shruta (Decoction): Coarse powder boiled with 4 to 16 parts of water reduced to 1/4th (dose: 2 Pala / 48 ml).
4. Hima (Cold Infusion): 1 part crushed herb soaked in 6 parts cold water overnight (dose: 2 Pala / 48 ml).
5. Phanta (Hot Infusion): 1 part herb steeped in 4 parts boiling water until warm (dose: 2 Pala / 48 ml).

The classical completion criteria during Sneha Paka (Taila and Ghrita preparation) are:
• Vartivat Kalka: The kalka residue can be rolled smoothly into a non-sticky wick between thumb and forefinger.
• Shabdahino Agnou: A pinch of kalka cast into open flame burns quietly without crackling sound, proving zero water presence.
• Phenodgama: Froth appears on the surface of medicated oils (Taila).
• Phenashanti: Froth subsides completely in medicated clarified butter (Ghrita).
• Gandha-Varna-Rasa Utpatti: Attainment of distinct medicinal aroma, hue, and taste according to the formulation.

In Sandhana Kalpana (natural biomedical fermentation):
• Arishta: Formulated using a boiled decoction (Kwatha) of raw herbal ingredients, which is filtered, sweetened with Jaggery (Guda), Honey (Madhu), or Sugar, inoculated with Dhataki flowers (Woodfordia fruticosa), and sealed in airtight containers for 30–60 days.
• Asava: Formulated without boiling; raw herbal powders are steeped directly in cold water or freshly expressed juices along with fermenting sugars and yeast inoculants. Asavas typically possess higher digestive-stimulant (Deepana) properties.

The 18 pharmaceutical refinements of Mercury outlined in Rasa Ratna Samucchaya are: 1. Svedana (thermal purification with alkaline drugs), 2. Mardana (intensive trituration), 3. Murchana (destruction of toxic globules), 4. Utthapana (restoration of luster), 5. Patana (distillation: Urdhwa, Adhah, Tiryak), 6. Rodhana (awakening intrinsic potency), 7. Niyamana (restraining motility), 8. Deepana (enhancing assimilation capacity), 9. Grahana, 10. Dharana, 11. Jarana (digestion of minerals), 12. Ranjana, 13. Sarana, 14. Kramana, 15. Vedhana, 16. Bhakshana, 17. Rasayana (internal therapy), and 18. Dehasiddhi (transmutation into cellular longevity).

Kupipakwa Rasayanas are sublimated mercurial formulations processed in glass bottles (Kachi Kupi) packed within a sand-bath (Valuka Yantra) with precise temperature curves (Mridu, Madhyama, and Tivragni). Based on deposition position inside the bottle, they are classified into:
• Kanthastha: Deposited at the neck of the bottle (e.g., Rasa Sindura, Makaradhwaja, Swarna Vanga).
• Talastha: Deposited at the bottom of the vessel (e.g., Sameerapannaga Rasa, Malla Sindura).
• Ubhayastha: Deposited at both neck and base (e.g., Hingulottha Parada, Manikya Rasa).

Under the NCISM revised syllabus for BAMS and MD/MS, examination questions are mapped directly to cognitive domain levels:
• K1 (Remember): Recall of classical shlokas, classifications, synonyms, and ingredients.
• K2 (Understand): Explaining reasons for specific purification media, shelf-life factors, and heating curves.
• K3 (Apply): Calculating drug proportions, executing laboratory SOPs, and selecting anupana.
• K4 (Analyze): Interpreting XRD/SEM spectra, differentiating adulterants, and troubleshooting batch defects.
• K5 (Evaluate): Assessing clinical efficacy, therapeutic margins of safety, and toxicity protocols.
• K6 (Create): Formulating new drug delivery systems and designing modern standard manufacturing processes.
